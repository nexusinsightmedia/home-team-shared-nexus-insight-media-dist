Best Project Management Software for 2025: We Tested 10 Tools So You Don't Have To

By Nexus Insight Media | Last Updated: May 2025

The Best Tools to Keep Your Team Organized

Project management software is the backbone of modern teamwork. Whether you're a solo freelancer, a growing startup, or an enterprise team, the right tool can be the difference between chaos and clarity. We spent three weeks testing the top platforms to find the best for every use case.

Best Overall: Notion

Rating: 9.5/10 Best for: Teams that want flexibility and power

Pricing

  • Free: $0 (unlimited pages, 7-day page history, up to 10 guests)
  • Plus: $10/month
  • Business: $18/month
  • Enterprise: Custom pricing

What We Love

  • Unbelievably flexible — it's a wiki, database, project manager, and notes app in one
  • Incredible database views (table, board, calendar, gallery, timeline, list)
  • AI writing assistant built-in ($10 add-on)
  • Powerful templates for any workflow
  • Great for both personal and team use
  • Excellent API and integrations

What Could Be Better

  • Steep learning curve for advanced features
  • No native time tracking (requires integrations)
  • Can become slow with very large databases
  • Mobile app is functional but not as good as desktop

Verdict

Notion is the Swiss Army knife of productivity tools. It does so much that it's hard to categorize. For small to medium teams that want one tool to rule them all, it's the best choice.

Best for Agile Teams: Linear

Rating: 9.5/10 Best for: Engineering teams, product teams, and startups

Pricing

  • Starter: $8/month per user
  • Business: $14/month per user
  • Enterprise: Custom pricing

What We Love

  • Blazing fast — the snappiest project management tool we tested
  • Keyboard-first design makes power users incredibly efficient
  • Beautiful, minimal interface
  • Excellent cycle-based workflows for agile teams
  • Roadmap view for stakeholder presentations
  • GitHub/GitLab integration is seamless

What Could Be Better

  • Designed primarily for software teams (less suited for marketing or HR)
  • Limited spreadsheet-style views
  • No native documentation/wiki feature
  • Smaller community/template library than Notion

Verdict

If you're a software team using agile or sprints, Linear is the best tool on the market. It's what you use when you've outgrown the complexity of Jira but need more structure than Trello.

Best for Visual Planning: Trello

Rating: 8.5/10 Best for: Simple workflows, visual thinkers, individuals

Pricing

  • Free: $0 (unlimited boards, 10 MB attachments, 1 Power-Up per board)
  • Standard: $5/month per user
  • Premium: $10/month per user
  • Enterprise: $17.50/month per user

What We Love

  • Incredibly intuitive — anyone can start using it in 5 minutes
  • Kanban boards are perfect for simple workflow tracking
  • Butler automation handles repetitive tasks
  • Huge library of Power-Ups (integrations)
  • Great for personal use (home projects, content calendars)

What Could Be Better

  • Limited for complex workflows
  • No native timeline/Gantt chart (Premium add-on)
  • Search is weak
  • Can get unwieldy with large boards

Verdict

Trello is the perfect entry-level project management tool. For simple workflows and personal use, nothing beats its simplicity. But growing teams will quickly hit its limits.

Best for Enterprise: Monday.com

Rating: 8.5/10 Best for: Large teams with complex workflows

Pricing

  • Basic: $9/month per user
  • Standard: $12/month per user
  • Pro: $19/month per user
  • Enterprise: Custom

What We Love

  • Extremely customizable views (board, timeline, calendar, Gantt, map)
  • Powerful automation engine with no-code triggers
  • Excellent reporting and dashboard features
  • Strong permissions and access controls
  • Integrates with 200+ tools

What Could Be Better

  • Can feel overwhelming with too many options
  • Gets expensive at scale
  • Some features feel like up-sells to higher tiers
  • Mobile app could be better

Verdict

Monday.com is the enterprise choice for a reason. It scales from a team of 5 to 500, and the automation capabilities save hours per week. If you need structure and reporting, this is your tool.

Which Tool Should You Choose?

  1. Notion — If you want one tool for docs, wikis, databases, and project management. Best for small to medium teams.
  2. Linear — If you're a software/product team doing agile. The fastest, most developer-friendly tool by far.
  3. Trello — If you just need simple task tracking. Perfect for solo freelancers or tiny teams.
  4. Monday.com — If you're a large organization that needs complex workflow automation and reporting.

Pro Tips

  • Start free — All of these tools have generous free tiers. Try 2-3 before committing.
  • Involve your team — The best tool is the one your team will actually use. Let them help decide.
  • Don't over-engineer — Start simple and add complexity as you grow. A tool that does everything can be paralyzing.
